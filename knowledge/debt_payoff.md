# Paying off debt: avalanche and snowball
Source: FinPilot financial-literacy notes, based on standard personal-finance practice and NCFE (National Centre for Financial Education) material.

## List your debts first
Write down every debt with its outstanding balance, interest rate and minimum monthly payment. Always pay the minimum on every debt so nothing goes overdue; the strategy only decides where any extra money goes.

## The avalanche method
Put every extra rupee toward the debt with the highest interest rate, while paying minimums on the rest. When it is cleared, move to the next-highest rate. Avalanche minimises total interest paid. Credit card balances at around 40% a year almost always come first, well ahead of personal loans at 11% to 16%.

## The snowball method
Put extra money toward the smallest balance first, regardless of rate. Clearing small debts quickly gives motivation and frees up their minimum payments. Snowball usually costs more interest than avalanche but some people stick with it better.

## Which to choose
If the difference in total interest is small, choose the method you will actually follow. If a credit card is revolving, clear it first under either method, because card interest is far higher than loan interest. Consider moving a large card balance into a lower-rate EMI or personal loan only if you stop adding new card spending.

## Prepaying a loan
Most floating-rate loans to individuals carry no prepayment penalty under RBI rules. Prepayment reduces the principal, which cuts future interest. Ask the lender whether to reduce the EMI or the tenure; reducing the tenure saves more interest.
