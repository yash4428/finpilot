# Income tax regimes in India: old vs new
Source: Summary of Income-tax Act provisions as amended by Finance Act 2025 (incometax.gov.in). Applies to FY 2025-26 and FY 2026-27 for resident individuals below 60.

## The new tax regime (default)
The new regime is the default from FY 2023-24 onward. Slab rates: income up to ₹4,00,000 is nil; ₹4,00,001 to ₹8,00,000 is 5%; ₹8,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 is 10%; ₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000 is 15%; ₹16,00,001 to ₹20,00,000 is 20%; ₹20,00,001 to ₹24,00,000 is 25%; above ₹24,00,000 is 30%. Salaried taxpayers get a standard deduction of ₹75,000.

## Rebate under section 87A in the new regime
If taxable income in the new regime does not exceed ₹12,00,000, a rebate of up to ₹60,000 wipes out the tax entirely. For a salaried person this means gross salary up to ₹12,75,000 pays no tax after the ₹75,000 standard deduction. Just above ₹12,00,000 of taxable income, marginal relief caps the tax so it is never more than the income exceeding ₹12,00,000.

## The old tax regime
Slab rates: up to ₹2,50,000 nil; ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000 is 5%; ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 is 20%; above ₹10,00,000 is 30%. The standard deduction for salaried taxpayers is ₹50,000. The 87A rebate of up to ₹12,500 applies only when taxable income is ₹5,00,000 or less. The old regime allows deductions such as 80C, 80D, 80CCD(1B), HRA exemption and home-loan interest under section 24(b).

## What the new regime does not allow
The new regime does not allow most exemptions and deductions: no 80C, no 80D, no HRA exemption, no LTA, and no deduction for interest on a self-occupied home loan. It does allow the standard deduction and the employer's contribution to NPS under section 80CCD(2).

## Health and education cess
A 4% health and education cess is added to the income tax (after rebate) in both regimes. Surcharge applies only above ₹50 lakh of income.

## How to choose a regime
Compare the total tax in both regimes using your actual deductions. The old regime tends to win only when deductions are large, for example a full ₹1.5 lakh in 80C, NPS, health insurance, a big HRA exemption and home-loan interest together. With the higher rebate and wider slabs since Budget 2025, most salaried people with modest deductions pay less under the new regime. Salaried individuals without business income can switch regimes every year when filing the return.
