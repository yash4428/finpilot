# Filing your income tax return (ITR)
Source: Income Tax Department e-filing guidance (incometax.gov.in).

## Which ITR form
ITR-1 (Sahaj) is for resident individuals with total income up to ₹50 lakh from salary or pension, one house property and other sources such as interest. Salaried people with capital gains beyond the small limit allowed in ITR-1, or with more than one house property, generally use ITR-2. Business or professional income needs ITR-3 or ITR-4.

## Documents to collect
Collect Form 16 from your employer, Form 26AS and the Annual Information Statement (AIS) from the e-filing portal, interest certificates from banks, and proofs of investments and premiums if you claim old-regime deductions. Match the TDS in Form 16 with Form 26AS before filing.

## Due dates
For individuals whose accounts do not need an audit, the usual due date is 31 July after the end of the financial year. A belated or revised return can generally be filed until 31 December. Filing late attracts a fee of up to ₹5,000 (₹1,000 if income is below ₹5 lakh) and interest on any unpaid tax.

## Verifying the return
A return is complete only after it is verified, within 30 days of filing. The easiest way is e-verification with an Aadhaar OTP, net banking or a bank account pre-validated on the portal.

## Choosing the regime when filing
Salaried taxpayers can choose the old or new regime each year in the return itself, even if they told their employer something different for TDS. If more tax is due at filing, pay it as self-assessment tax before submitting.
