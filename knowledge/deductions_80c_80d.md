# Tax-saving deductions: 80C, 80D, NPS and others
Source: Summary of Chapter VI-A deductions, Income-tax Act (incometax.gov.in). These deductions apply only under the old tax regime unless stated otherwise.

## Section 80C: up to ₹1,50,000
Section 80C allows a combined deduction of up to ₹1,50,000 a year. Eligible items include employee provident fund (EPF) and voluntary PF, Public Provident Fund (PPF), Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS), life insurance premiums, National Savings Certificates (NSC), 5-year tax-saver fixed deposits, Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana, principal repayment of a home loan, and tuition fees for up to two children.

## Choosing an 80C instrument
ELSS mutual funds have the shortest lock-in of 3 years and invest in equity, so returns vary. PPF has a 15-year tenure with government-backed, tax-free interest. Tax-saver FDs lock money for 5 years and the interest is taxable. Many salaried people already use a large part of the limit through EPF, so check your payslip before investing more.

## Section 80D: health insurance
Section 80D allows a deduction for health insurance premiums: up to ₹25,000 for self, spouse and children (₹50,000 if the insured person is a senior citizen), plus a separate up to ₹25,000 for parents (₹50,000 if they are senior citizens). Preventive health check-ups up to ₹5,000 count within these limits.

## Section 80CCD(1B): extra ₹50,000 for NPS
Contributions to the National Pension System (NPS) Tier-1 account qualify for an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B), over and above the ₹1,50,000 of 80C. NPS money is largely locked until age 60.

## Section 80CCD(2): employer contribution to NPS
The employer's contribution to your NPS account is deductible under 80CCD(2). This is one of the few deductions also allowed in the new regime, up to 14% of basic salary plus DA for private-sector employees.

## Section 80TTA: savings account interest
Interest from savings bank accounts is deductible up to ₹10,000 a year under 80TTA in the old regime. Senior citizens instead get up to ₹50,000 under 80TTB on deposit interest.

## Section 24(b): home loan interest
Interest on a loan for a self-occupied house is deductible up to ₹2,00,000 a year under section 24(b) in the old regime.

## How to save tax legally as a salaried employee
Start by checking which regime is cheaper for you. Under the old regime, use the full ₹1,50,000 of section 80C (EPF already counts toward it), add up to ₹50,000 in NPS under 80CCD(1B), buy health insurance for yourself and your parents under 80D, and claim HRA if you pay rent. Under the new regime most of these do not reduce tax, but asking your employer to route part of your salary into NPS under 80CCD(2) still does. Do not invest in a product only for the deduction; choose it for the goal and treat the tax saving as a bonus.
