# Budgeting and emergency funds
Source: FinPilot financial-literacy notes, based on NCFE and SEBI investor-education material.

## The 50/30/20 budget
A simple starting point divides take-home pay into 50% for needs (rent, groceries, utilities, EMIs), 30% for wants (dining out, shopping, entertainment) and 20% for savings and investments. Adjust the split to your city and life stage; the point is to decide the split before the month starts.

## Tracking spending by category
Setting a monthly limit per category and checking it weekly catches overspending early. If a category is ahead of pace in the middle of the month, cut back for the rest of the month rather than waiting for the total at month end.

## Emergency fund
Keep three to six months of essential expenses as an emergency fund before investing for long-term goals. Hold it somewhere safe and quickly accessible, such as a savings account, a sweep-in fixed deposit or a liquid mutual fund. Rebuild it after any withdrawal.

## Saving for a goal
For a goal less than three years away, prefer low-risk options such as recurring deposits, fixed deposits or liquid and short-duration debt funds. Divide the remaining amount by the months left to get a monthly target and automate it on salary day.

## Subscriptions and recurring costs
Recurring charges such as streaming services, gym memberships and app subscriptions add up quietly. Review them every few months and cancel the ones you do not use. Annual plans are cheaper only if you would keep the service for the whole year anyway.
