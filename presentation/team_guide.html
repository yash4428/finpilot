1. The idea in one minute

FinPilot is a personal-finance app with an AI assistant. It puts your credit cards, loans, EMIs, spending, budgets, savings goals and tax in one place. Its main promise: it tells you how much money is actually yours to spend.

The problem it solves. Think of a salaried person in India with two credit cards, a personal loan, a laptop EMI and a few UPI apps. Their bank app shows ₹1,48,520, so they feel rich. But a big part of that money is already promised to card bills and EMIs due this month. So they overspend, miss due dates, pay late fees and interest, and don't notice subscriptions going up. And if they ask an AI chatbot "can I afford this?", it answers confidently with numbers it made up.

FinPilot does three things:
  1. One dashboard for all your money.
  2. Safe-to-spend: your real spendable money, not just your balance.
  3. Pilot, the AI assistant: ask questions in English, Hinglish or Hindi, by typing or by voice. It answers from your real data and is never allowed to make up a number.

If you remember one line, remember this: "FinPilot tells you what's actually yours to spend, and its AI never makes up a number."

2. How it's built, in simple words

Think of it like a restaurant. The customer only talks to the waiter. The waiter talks to the kitchen. The kitchen has a manager who keeps all the records, and a specialist who does the clever work.

React websiteWhat you see and click. The "waiter".
Java Spring BootThe main backend. Login, all the money rules, reminders. The "manager".
PostgreSQLThe database where all the data is saved. The "record book".
Python FastAPIThe second backend. Machine-learning models and the AI assistant. The "specialist".
Google GeminiThe AI language model that writes Pilot's answers.

The course required two backends, which is why there's both Java and Python. The website only ever talks to Java. Java asks Python whenever it needs machine learning or AI.

The most important design choice When Pilot needs a number (your balance, your bills), it does not guess. It calls a "tool" that fetches the real number from the Java backend, using that user's own login. Then our code checks every number in the answer against that data. If the AI invents a number, the answer is thrown away and rewritten.

3. The features, one by one

For each feature: what it is, where to find it in the app, how to show it, and one line to say.

Safe-to-spend (the headline feature)

Instead of one bank balance, FinPilot shows two numbers. Safe to spend now is your balance minus every card bill and EMI due this month. Predicted on the 30th also subtracts recurring bills (rent, Netflix, electricity) and your usual daily spending. A machine-learning model predicts the daily spending from your history.

Where
Overview page, the big dark card at the top.
Show it
Point at the strip: each bill peels away from the balance, and the gold part at the end is "yours to keep". Click See the full breakdown to show the line-by-line math.
Say it
"Your bank balance isn't your spending money. FinPilot subtracts what's already committed and shows what's really yours."

"Thinking of buying something?" and the EMI advisor

Type a price and FinPilot instantly says if it fits: green (fine), gold (tight) or red (too much). If it doesn't fit, it suggests a smarter way to pay, like a 6-month no-cost EMI.

Where
Inside the same dark card on Overview.
Show it
Type 20000 (fits), then 90000 (red, with a "Smarter: … EMI" line). Or drag the slider.
Say it
"Before you buy, it tells you if you can afford it, and if not, a better way to pay."

Spend today (daily allowance)

How much you can spend today and still save 10% of your income this month.

Where
Top-right corner of the safe-to-spend card.
Say it
"A daily budget that protects your savings automatically."

FinPilot score (financial health out of 100)

One score made of four parts, 25 points each: savings rate, debt load (how much income goes to EMIs), safety net (how many months of spending your savings cover) and discipline (card usage, overdue bills, budgets). It also says what to fix next.

Where
Overview, top-right card with the green ring.
Say it
"Like a fitness score, but for your money, with a tip on what to improve."

Spending calendar and charts

A heatmap of the last 13 weeks (brighter = more spending), a weekday bar chart ("you spend most on Sundays"), this month vs last month, and a donut of where the money went.

Where
Overview, scroll down.
Show it
Hover over days on the calendar and segments of the donut.

Autopay detective

FinPilot automatically finds repeating payments (subscriptions and bills), shows what they cost per year, and catches price rises. In the demo, Spotify went up by ₹20 and it's flagged.

Where
Overview, the "Autopay detective" card next to the calendar.
Say it
"It finds subscriptions you forgot about and warns you when prices go up."

Transactions: auto-categorising, CSV import, unusual charges

Every transaction gets a category automatically (Dining, Groceries, Rent…). It uses simple rules plus a machine-learning model, and it learns when you correct a category. You can upload a bank statement as a CSV file. A second machine-learning model flags unusual charges and always explains why ("looks like a duplicate", "40× your usual shopping").

Where
Transactions page (left menu).
Show it
Click the Flagged chip to see the unusual charges with reasons. Change a category from its dropdown. Click Import CSV → Download a sample statement → import it. It shows "11 imported, 11 categorised, 1 flagged".
Say it
"It sorts your spending by itself and flags anything suspicious, like a duplicate Swiggy order."

Cards, loans and bank accounts

All your credit cards (with how much of the limit is used), loans with how many EMIs are left, and bank accounts with balances. When you add a loan, the monthly EMI is calculated for you.

Where
Cards & loans page.
Show it
Click Add card and set the due date 3 days from today. Then open the Dues page and the bell icon: the new bill and a reminder appear.

Dues and reminders

Every card bill and EMI on one timeline. A scheduled job runs every morning at 8 AM, marks bills as "due soon" or "overdue", and sends reminders 5 days before. Marking an EMI paid schedules next month's automatically.

Where
Dues page, plus the bell icon (top-right) for alerts.
Show it
Click Mark paid on an EMI. It moves to "Recently paid" and next month's EMI appears.
Say it
"You never miss a due date again."

Budgets

A monthly limit per category. Each bar has a small marker showing where you should be at this point in the month, so you notice overspending early. Alerts fire at 80% and 100%.

Where
Budgets page (and a summary on Overview).

Goals on autopilot

Savings goals (emergency fund, a trip). FinPilot works out how much to save each month to reach each goal and checks it against what you have left every month. "Add money" moves money from the bank into the goal.

Where
Goals page.

Tax

Compares the old and new tax regimes using the real rules (tax slabs, standard deduction, the 87A rebate, 4% cess) and shows every step. Sliders for 80C, health insurance (80D), NPS and HRA change the tax live. Also shows ways to save and a return-filing checklist. It's an estimate, and it says so.

Where
Tax page.
Show it
Drag the 80C slider: the old regime's tax drops, but the new regime still wins for the demo user.

Credit health

A simulated credit score (300–900, like CIBIL) based on card usage and on-time payments, with tips. It's clearly labelled as simulated; we don't connect to a real credit bureau.

Where
Overview, "Credit health" card.

Pilot, the AI assistant (chat)

Ask anything about your money in plain language. Pilot works in steps: understand the question → plan which tools to use → fetch real data → calculate in code → write the answer → check every number. For advice questions ("What is 80C?") it searches 8 trusted documents about tax and finance and cites the source as [1] [2]. That search technique is called RAG.

Where
"Ask Pilot" in the left menu, or press ⌘K anywhere.
Show it
Ask "Can I afford a ₹1,00,000 laptop this month?" You get a verdict card and an EMI comparison table. Point at the green line "N figures traced to your data", then click How Pilot worked this out to show every step.
Also try "What is 80C?" (look for the [1] [2] citations) and "Which debt should I pay off first?"
Say it
"It's an AI agent, not just a chatbot. It looks up your real data and our code checks every number before you see it."

4. The wow moment: talk to Pilot in Hinglish

Press the gold mic button (bottom-right of every page) and just speak, in English, Hinglish or Hindi.

"Kya main dedh lakh ki bike le sakta hoon?"

Pilot answers out loud, something like: "Abhi nahi. ₹72,965 kam pad rahe hain. Lekin 6 mahine ki no-cost EMI pe ₹25,000 mahina, wo aaram se chal jayega." At the same moment the dashboard reacts: the safe-to-spend strip carves out the bike's price and the shortfall turns red.

Other things to say into the mic:

  • "Mera bill kab bharna hai?" → the "Coming up" bills section glows.
  • "Pichle mahine khane pe kitna kharcha kiya?" → the spending chart glows.
  • "Tax kaise bachaye?" → it jumps to the Tax page.
If the mic doesn't work in the room The voice screen has ready-made question buttons and an "Or type here" box. Tapping one gives exactly the same answer, spoken reply and dashboard reaction. Voice works in Chrome, needs internet, and the browser asks for mic permission the first time.

5. Running the live demo

The project runs on the project owner's laptop. If you're showing it live:

  1. Make sure the database is running: brew services start postgresql@16
  2. Open Terminal in the project folder and run ./run.sh --seed (it loads fresh demo data and prints the link).
  3. Open the printed link in Chrome. It's usually http://localhost:5175, not 5173, because another project uses 5173.
  4. Click Explore the demo account (email demo@finpilot.app, password demo1234).
About the AI key Pilot uses Google Gemini when an API key is set in the project's .env file. Without a key, or when Google's servers are busy, Pilot automatically switches to its offline mode: same tools, same real numbers, simpler wording. Nothing breaks, so the demo works either way. In "How Pilot worked this out", offline mode shows as "Deterministic planner".

No laptop with the project? Just present the slides. Every slide has a real screenshot, and the script for each slide is in the speaker notes.

RAGRetrieval-augmented generation: before answering an advice question, Pilot searches our trusted documents and answers only from them, with citations.
FAISSA fast search index (a vector database) that stores our documents for RAG.
Grounding checkOur code checks that every number in Pilot's answer came from real data. If not, the answer is rejected.

7. Questions the teacher might ask

Why two backends?
The course required it. It also keeps things clean: Java is the system of record (accounts, rules, data), Python does the machine learning and AI. The website only talks to Java.
How do you stop the AI from making up numbers?
Three ways. The AI must get numbers from tools that read the real database. Calculations like "can I afford it" are done in our code, not by the AI. And after the AI writes an answer, our code checks every number against the real data; if one doesn't match, the answer is rejected and rewritten.
Did you train or fine-tune the AI?
No. We use Google Gemini as-is. We only use RAG (searching our documents) and tool calling. Training, fine-tuning and RAG are three different things, and we only do RAG.
Which machine-learning models did you use?
Logistic Regression with TF-IDF for categorising transactions, Linear Regression for forecasting spending, and Isolation Forest for spotting unusual charges. All from scikit-learn.
Why not connect to real bank accounts?
In India that needs an RBI Account Aggregator licence, which a student project can't get. CSV bank-statement import is the legitimate alternative, and it's what real users can do.
Is the credit score real?
No, it's simulated from card usage and payment history, and the app labels it "Simulated".
What about privacy?
We only store the last 4 digits of cards. Before anything goes to the AI, emails, card numbers, account numbers, PAN and Aadhaar numbers are masked. Every request is tied to the logged-in user, so nobody sees anyone else's data.
Is it tested?
Yes, 30 automated tests across Java and Python (tax math, EMI formula, ML models, the AI loop, Hinglish understanding and more).
How does it understand Hinglish?
Our code converts Hinglish and Hindi phrases ("le sakta hoon", "bees hazaar", "pichle mahine") into simple English meanings before deciding what you're asking. The spoken reply is built from the real numbers.
What happens if the AI service is down?
Pilot retries, tries a backup Gemini model, and then falls back to a rule-based mode that uses the same tools and real numbers. The user always gets a correct answer.

8. What's not done (say this honestly if asked)

  • No real bank linking. CSV import instead (RBI licence needed).
  • Credit score is simulated, not from a bureau.
  • The Docker setup is written but wasn't tested; the app was run with the run.sh script instead.
  • Document search uses a simple word-matching method (TF-IDF) to turn text into vectors, not a neural embedding model.
  • Tax covers salaried people under 60 (no capital gains or surcharge).
  • Reminders are in-app only, not SMS or email yet.

Future plans (last slide): bank linking through Account Aggregator, a mobile app, SMS/email reminders, shared family budgets, more Indian languages.
